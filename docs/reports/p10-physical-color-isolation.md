## What the two scores share

Count zero-based viewer columns `x=0..15` and rows `y=0..63` from the full board's upper-left, including dark margins. Record the game mode and score, not just the colored spots. Under the [verified portrait mapping](../specs/03-display-rendering.md#2-physical-canvas-and-hardware):

| Quantity | Map from viewer `(x,y)` |
| --- | --- |
| Installed module | upper / expected controller-near if `y<32`; lower / expected downstream otherwise (confirm J1/J2 if visible) |
| Module-local shift coordinate | `31 - (y % 32)` |
| Zero-based source clock in each LAT interval | `rawX = 63 - y` (first block shifts to the far module, second stays near) |
| Binary ABC address | `x % 8` (`A` is the LSB) |
| RGB bus group | `RGB1` for `x<8`, otherwise `RGB2` |

In **Doubles**, score bands are `y=1..14` and `17..30`, both on the **upper physical module**. Matched glyph positions at identical `x` share ABC, RGB bank and shift index modulo 16 but are 16 clocks apart. Example: `(4,6)` and `(4,22)` are at raw shift positions `57` and `41`, both address `4`, both RGB1. This is not yet evidence of a 16-output chip or a second bad panel. In **Singles**, score bands are `8..21` and `27..40`; the second score straddles the seam and matched positions are 19 source clocks apart. In either mode, `x` and `x+8` share ABC but use different RGB buses; `y` and `y+32` share a module-local shift coordinate but are on different modules. Compare only positions known to be commanded on, and log nearby healthy on-pixels as controls. Similar-looking glyphs can make unrelated faults look periodic.

For an unchanged held score: if the existing photos are unavailable, take two square-on originals of the full face several seconds apart with focus/exposure locked and highlights not clipped (one ~1/15 s or longer if possible). Mark which pixels also look wrong by eye. No app interaction or equipment access is needed. Use a record per pixel/frame:

```text
image_or_trace_id,mode_or_bench_phase,score_or_color,viewer_x,viewer_y,expected,observed,by_eye,module,local_shift,ABC,RGB_bank
```

Do not infer a channel pass/fail from a saturated photo: magenta, cyan and blue *suggest* weak/missing G, R and R+G respectively, but verify with held primaries at the same coordinates.

## What the installed ESP32 actually requests

- `firmware/src/display_render.cpp` uses SHIFTREG / TYPE138, single DMA buffer, brightness 12, `HZ_8M`, `clkphase=true`. HUB75 DMA 3.0.15's `HZ_10M` is an alias of `HZ_8M` (both 8,000,000). Changing that enum spelling cannot change the clock.
- The [original ESP32 TRM §22.5](https://www.espressif.com/sites/default/files/documentation/esp32_technical_reference_manual_en.pdf) specifies PLL_F160M_CLK when APLL is disabled and WS at half BCK in LCD TX mode. The archived ELF's `Bus_Parallel16::init()` loads `2` and `4` in its divider branch (`0x400d8e70` area); pinned backend selects `CLKM_DIV_NUM=4` for the request, fraction `0/1`, and `TX_BCK_DIV_NUM=2`. So I2S1 WS, routed to GPIO16, is predicted at **160 MHz / 4 / 2 / 2 = 10 MHz** (100 ns). A divider of 5 predicts **8 MHz** (125 ns). These are programmed values and predictions, **not** receiver measurements. With the linked schedule of 135 transfers per address × 64 clocks × 8 addresses (transition bit 0), the rates are ~145 Hz and ~116 Hz respectively, excluding any real bus gaps. The library estimates refresh from the requested 8 MHz. Do not change the request or color depth for the divider-only experiment; doing so could change the DMA schedule.
- Phase true inverts only GPIO-matrix WS/CLK; it does not re-time the data or control words. The original ESP32 driver's FIFO word-pair swap is a memory order correction, not an instruction to swap paired bits in a captured wire trace. Neither phase label guarantees rising-edge setup/hold at the panel. Measure at the receiving IC's specified sampling edge and VIH/VIL, including R/G/B lanes separately and extra CLK threshold crossings from ringing. An all-white fill hides adjacent-sample errors; use sparse transitions.
- At the source, LAT is in the last of the 64 words. The first bitplane shifts the next row while ABC still addresses the previous row; after its LAT, ABC changes to the current address. OE is active low. At brightness 12 and transition bit 0 the source sets `brightness_in_x_pixels` to 1 or 2; the center-window rounding for **both** yields `[31,33)`, so the expected OE-low span is two words. The later `setBrightnessOE()` rewrites OE over the whole row. A `latch_blanking` change 2→3 still yields the same OE slots at these settings; **it is not a discriminating blanking test**. Compare the last CLK and LAT at both receivers and ABC/OE around an actual row change (include 7→0); for any later OE experiment prove the waveform changed first. At nominal 10 MHz an OE-low pulse is only ~200 ns, so an average voltage or a low-rate logic capture cannot characterize it.
- [Upstream #816](https://github.com/mrcodetastic/ESP32-HUB75-MatrixPanel-DMA/issues/816) shows a divider-5 workaround for a Tidbyt Gen2 with the same ESP32 silicon revision, but a different panel and pattern-dependent red artifacts. [Release 3.1.0](https://github.com/mrcodetastic/ESP32-HUB75-MatrixPanel-DMA/blob/3.1.0/src/platforms/esp32/esp32_i2s_parallel_dma.cpp) and inspected master `7f5e9cf8` retain the original-ESP32 2/4 branch; upgrading is not this fix. [#49](https://github.com/mrcodetastic/ESP32-HUB75-MatrixPanel-DMA/issues/49) has scope results for older drivers; [#134](https://github.com/mrcodetastic/ESP32-HUB75-MatrixPanel-DMA/issues/134) shows panel-specific phase and ghosting fixes; neither proves a solution for these exact modules. No matching panel model/driver IC has been established. A pure R/G pin permutation does not corrupt `R=G=B` white, though a bad pin/connection or misidentified driver/scan protocol can.

### Interpret the first failure before choosing a repair

| Failed check / distinguishing evidence | Next intervention (separate authorization) |
| --- | --- |
| Same die loses one primary under two lit phases, including after the neighbors change, with healthy same-bank peers and clean data/control/power | On the bench, compare the suspect module with a known-good same-revision module at the **same** receiver position/input and branch. If the fault follows the marked coordinates on the module, replace that module. One site points to a die, joint or local trace; several addresses sharing a particular output group point to the constant-current driver/output net. Use IC markings and continuity to identify the net, not presumed 8/16-channel grouping. |
| Missing bit follows light/dark transitions, or adjacent dark position gets the missing channel; repeats on an RGB lane or appears/worsens only at far J1 | Capture CLK + failing RGB and a healthy lane at controller, near J1, near J2, and far J1 when accessible, allowing for the 32-clock shift-through delay. Bad between J2 and far J1: substitute one short verified ribbon and repeat; reseat/replace the implicated keyed connector/ribbon if substitution clears it and return reproduces. Bad at J2 with clean near J1: investigate near-module forwarding/buffer path. Clean logic-analyzer bits alone do not show analog timing margin. |
| Receiver threshold or ground margin fails while the ESP32 drive looks clean | Read the actual input-buffer markings and supply. For example, TI [SN74HC245](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74hc245.pdf) requires VIH ≥3.15 V at 4.5 V and ≥4.2 V at 6 V; a direct 3.3 V signal into a 5 V HC part is not a guaranteed interface. TI [SN74AHCT245](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74ahct245.pdf) is TTL-input (VIH ≥2.0 V at 4.5–5.5 V). If needed, compare a correctly engineered unidirectional 3.3→5 V buffer driving the identified 5 V inputs, with reset/OE safety and common ground. No 5 V returns to ESP32 GPIO, no strap pull-up changes, no long new jumpers. If cable/ringing is implicated, repair the return/ribbon first. |
| Fault depends on `a` (both) vs `n` or `f` with the same lit coordinates on the viewed module and coincides with rail sag/ground movement | Check regulated DC across each J1 module's separate **power connector** (+5 V to its own GND) and DC return drop to the source under black and loaded masks; scope ripple/dips synchronized to OE at both modules. Isolation also changes switching data, so load-sensitivity alone is insufficient. The historical ~5.06 V doesn't clear transient droop. Repair the measured branch connector/return or supply path; retain proper fuses/current limits and never raise above the module's rated voltage. Use correctly referenced probes; never lift protective earth or probe mains. |
| Adjacent-row/previous-color ghost or a whole address is duplicated; bad LAT/ABC/OE timing | Check the decoded address relative to LAT, OE and actual optical row, not just ABC while shifting. Confirm exact line-decoder/driver datasheets and control integrity first. Trial blanking only with a demonstrably wider disabled interval, and never change driver/scan type by guessing from “P10”. |
| CLK/data setup or pulse-width margin is insufficient with correct voltage levels and verified cabling | Run **A/B/A** on the same spare: stock divider 4 → divider 5 → stock 4. Patch only the pinned original-ESP32 backend in an isolated bench dependency, not the production cache; keep the request at `HZ_8M`, phase, buffer, pins, pattern, blanking, brightness and cabling fixed. Verify register readback and measured 100/125/100 ns CLK periods. Compare the same failing coordinates, next dark neighbor and electrical margin. If the edge rather than frequency is implicated, compare phase true/false/true at fixed divider instead. Do not try both changes together. Improvement at 8 MHz without a voltage/edge/timing check is a workaround, not a root-cause diagnosis. |

## Acceptance and verification

Before/after evidence must include repeatable fault coordinates with phase and primary, original photos/by-eye notes, scope or substitution evidence localizing the failure, and a return-to-baseline where safe. On the repaired setup, witness all eight white masks covering both panels, the failing locations in each primary, and no light in black; then verify clean white scores in both modes (including a Singles score spanning the seam), cold and after warm-up, with normal display/BLE/Undo/OTA health intact as applicable. Court 1 itself is not accepted until its **physical** display passes after a separately approved intervention.

Software-only this session: four isolated ESP32 diagnostic builds; host mask/command tests (all 1,024 positions, both RGB banks, both panel blocks, bounded load); 200,512 existing boundary checks; 109 production firmware host tests and all 72 pixel snapshots. The latter guard against unrelated changes, not against bad emitted colors. No production correction or separately signed OTA candidate is warranted before the fault is localized. If firmware is indicated, keep the production fix restricted to the proven display setting/backend, build and verify separately signed A/B-slot-compatible artifacts, and obtain approval before any erase or install. Any OTA upload can overwrite the current fallback; do not use Court 1 to test candidates. Local evidence: `20261120-pi-p10-color-isolation/` under the project session artifacts.
